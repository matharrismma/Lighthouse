# Public-Domain Curriculum — "learn here when you have nothing else"

A curated catalog of the best **public-domain** texts across every subject, to
build a free, offline, ownable education (the longest lever for the poor). These
are *real, time-tested* texts — the Bumblebee discipline applied to curriculum:
recombine found, attributed, true content; never generate a lesson and pass it
off as sound.

## The load-bearing principle: a timeless spine + a verified present

Old texts are **not uniformly better** — that would be laundering nostalgia. The
honest split:

- **Timeless (old often genuinely better)** — reading *method*, mathematics,
  classical languages, logic & rhetoric, literature, penmanship, primary-source
  history, Scripture, music theory. The old books are systematic, rigorous, and
  expect mastery. (The mid-century switch away from systematic phonics was
  documented as a failure — Flesch's *Why Johnny Can't Read*, 1955 — and the
  modern science of reading vindicated the old approach.)
- **Verify-or-replace (the world moved)** — modern science, geography facts,
  recent history, technology, medicine/health. Here the old text is for
  *foundation and method only*; **current facts come from the verified engine.**

So the architecture is: **public-domain texts give the timeless spine; the
Concordance engine supplies and corrects what changed.** Deep *and* current.

## Sources (where the texts live)

- [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/) — 60k+ clean plain-text/HTML PD books; best for ingestion.
- [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/) — 30M+ scans, every subject, no login.
- [HathiTrust](https://www.hathitrust.org/), [Standard Ebooks](https://standardebooks.org/) (carefully formatted), [DPLA](https://dp.la/), [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/), [Wikisource](https://wikisource.org/).

## The discipline (apply to every entry before it ships)

1. **Confirm PD status + the exact edition.** US rule: published **before 1931** = public domain (95-year term, as of 2026). Government works (e.g., USDA bulletins) are PD regardless. Some digitizations add a CC license — honor it.
2. **Flag dated facts and period bias** — old science/geography/history carry outdated facts and period attitudes (racial, colonial, cultural). Surface honestly; correct with the verified engine.
3. **Health is reference, never advice** — old medical *treatment* can be dangerous. Anatomy/structure is durable; treatment is not.
4. **Non-English reading content needs a native-literacy reviewer** (see [the multilingual reading bundle](../tools/build_learning_bundle.py)).
5. **Always attribute.** Title, author, edition, source.

## By subject

### Reading & literacy  ·  *timeless — start here*
- **McGuffey's Eclectic Readers** (W.H. McGuffey, 1879 rev.) — graded Primer→Sixth. The anchor.
- **Webster's Elementary Spelling Book** ("Blue-Backed Speller," Noah Webster) — systematic syllabary.
- **The New England Primer** (1700s) — earliest; archaic + heavily catechetical (historical use).
- Baldwin's, Elson, Beacon readers (pre-1929 eds).

### Spelling, grammar & composition  ·  *timeless*
- **Reed & Kellogg**, *Graded / Higher Lessons in English* (1870s–80s) — sentence diagramming.
- Webster's Speller (spelling); Barrett Wendell, *English Composition*.

### Penmanship  ·  *timeless*
- **Spencerian Penmanship** copybooks (P.R. Spencer); **Palmer Method** (A.N. Palmer, pre-1929 eds).

### Mathematics  ·  *timeless*
- **Ray's Arithmetic** series (Joseph Ray, "Ray's New Arithmetics," 1877) + **Ray's Algebra**.
- **Euclid's Elements** — Todhunter, Playfair, or Heath translations; **Byrne's colored Euclid** (1847) is extraordinary.
- **Hall & Knight**, *Elementary Algebra* / *Higher Algebra*; **Wentworth** geometry; **Todhunter** trigonometry & calculus.
- Context: De Morgan, *On the Study and Difficulties of Mathematics*; Cajori, *A History of Mathematics*.

### Logic & rhetoric  ·  *timeless*
- **Jevons**, *Elementary Lessons in Logic* (1870); **Whately**, *Elements of Logic* / *Elements of Rhetoric*; Aristotle in translation.

### Classical languages  ·  *timeless*
- **Latin:** Allen & Greenough, *New Latin Grammar* (1903, PD); D'Ooge, *Latin for Beginners* (1909).
- **Greek:** Smyth, *Greek Grammar* (1920); White, *First Greek Book*; Liddell & Scott lexicon (older eds).

### Literature & the classics  ·  *timeless — the richest trove*
- Shakespeare, Dickens, Austen, Twain (pre-1929), Bunyan's *Pilgrim's Progress*, Defoe; Homer/Virgil/Plutarch in translation; Aesop, Grimm, Andersen; Palgrave's *Golden Treasury* (poetry). Gutenberg overflows here.

### History & civics  ·  *primary sources timeless; old textbooks verify*
- **Primary sources (excellent):** Plutarch's *Lives*, Herodotus, Thucydides, Gibbon's *Decline and Fall*; the Declaration, Constitution, *Federalist Papers*; de Tocqueville, *Democracy in America*.
- **Old narrative textbooks** (Guerber's *Story of…* series, Dickens' *A Child's History of England*) — engaging, but **dated + period bias**; use for narrative, verify facts.

### Geography  ·  *verify-or-replace (heavily dated)*
- Old geography texts are valuable as *maps-as-history* and method only — borders, names, and populations have all changed. **Current facts from the verified engine.**

### Science  ·  *foundational/classical timeless; modern from the engine*
- **Durable:** Faraday, *The Chemical History of a Candle* (superb demonstrations); Euclid (geometry); Newton's *Principia* (advanced); Darwin, *On the Origin of Species*; Kingsley, *Madam How and Lady Why* (nature study).
- **Caveat:** no modern physics, chemistry, genetics, cosmology — those come from the **verified engine**, not a 19th-century text.

### Health & the body  ·  *reference, never advice — strongest caveat*
- **Gray's Anatomy** (1858 / 1918 eds) for anatomical *structure*. Old *treatment* is unsafe — current medical guidance only, and always "reference, not advice."

### Music  ·  *timeless*
- PD harmony/theory texts; PD hymnals (the engine already holds a hymn shelf).

### Practical arts, trades, home & agriculture  ·  *mixed*
- **USDA Farmers' Bulletins** (PD government works — much of the basics on gardening, food preservation, soil, livestock is durable).
- Hand-tool carpentry & handcraft manuals (hand methods timeless; powered/chemical tech dated); Lydia Child, *The American Frugal Housewife*.

### Bible & Christian classics  ·  *the engine already holds much of this*
- KJV (PD) + the 22 translations already in the keeping; Bunyan, *à Kempis* (*Imitation of Christ*), Foxe's *Book of Martyrs*, the Church Fathers, the creeds & Westminster catechism (already in the substrate).

## Roadmap — what to ingest first (into [the offline bundle](../tools/build_learning_bundle.py))

1. **Reading:** McGuffey Primer + First Reader (English on-ramp; replaces the hand-authored phonics with the time-tested original). *Timeless.*
2. **Arithmetic:** Ray's Primary Arithmetic. *Timeless.*
3. **Geometry:** Euclid Book I (with Byrne's figures). *Timeless.*
4. **Literature/character:** Aesop, *Pilgrim's Progress* (already partly in the keeping). *Timeless.*
5. **Civics:** the Founding documents (Constitution already in the keeping). *Timeless.*
6. Each tagged **timeless** (ship as-is, attributed) or **verify** (pair with the engine; flag dated facts). Non-English reading content waits for a native-literacy reviewer.

The aim: a person with **nothing else** can open one offline file and get a real,
ordered education — reading, sums, geometry, the classics, the founding of
nations, and the Word — free, ownable, and unable to be cut off. The old masters
teach the timeless; the engine keeps it current and honest; and the whole of it
points beyond itself, to Christ.

## Famous textbooks in other languages

A reading program must fit each language's own writing system (see the discipline
above) — these are the celebrated, historically load-bearing primers per language
to anchor each track. Many are already public domain; each still needs a
**native-literacy reviewer + a PD-edition check** before it ships, and several
carry a period worldview to surface honestly.

- **Russian** — **Tolstoy, *Azbuka* / *Novaya Azbuka*** (1872/1875): Tolstoy wrote his own ABC — reading, writing, counting, original short stories, teacher's notes; still Russia's most-reprinted primer. **Ushinsky, *Rodnoe Slovo* ("Native Word")** (1864): the first mass Russian reading textbook, phonemic method + teacher's guide. Both PD.
- **Chinese** — the **"San Bai Qian"** triad: **Three Character Classic** (三字经, 13th c.), **Thousand Character Classic** (千字文), **Hundred Family Surnames** (百家姓). The universal literacy texts for centuries (~2,000 characters across the three). PD. *Note:* Chinese literacy is character-recognition, not phonics — a different paradigm, bridged today via pinyin/zhuyin; Confucian-morality worldview.
- **French** — **G. Bruno (Mme Fouillée), *Le Tour de la France par deux enfants*** (1877): the iconic French reader — two orphans journey across France; reading + geography + civics + virtue. PD ([Gutenberg #27782](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/27782)); carries post-1870 nationalist framing — flag. **Méthode Boscher** (1906): the celebrated syllabic method — *verify PD* (French term is life+70).
- **Spanish** — the **cartilla / silabario** tradition (19th-c. eds PD; e.g. *Silabario de Idioma Mexicano*). The famous modern *Silabario Hispanoamericano* (Dufflocq, 1945) is likely still in copyright — **do not use**; build from PD cartillas + a reviewer. (Our hand-authored Spanish starter already follows this method.)
- **German** — the **Fibel** primer tradition; Hoffmann's *Der Struwwelpeter* (1845, PD) — famous cautionary tales (period attitudes — flag; not a reader proper).
- **Japanese** — the **Iroha** (いろは), the classic pangram poem that orders the kana — the traditional kana on-ramp. PD.
- **Arabic** — the traditional **Qāʿida Nūrāniyya** approach (letter-forms → connected script → Quranic reading); RTL (the shell supports it); native reviewer essential.
- **Hindi / Devanagari** — the **Barakhadi** (बारहखड़ी) syllabary-matrix (consonant × vowel) — the standard abugida on-ramp.
- **Latin / Greek** — see *Classical languages* above (Allen & Greenough, D'Ooge; Smyth, White).
